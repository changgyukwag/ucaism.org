Redress Delay, Platform Contestability, and Ex-Ante Fiduciary Agency

Abstract

Digital platforms can create substantial coordination value while sustaining terms that users find difficult to contest. This paper develops the Extraction Gap, a temporal mechanism linking immediate platform extraction to delayed economic resistance. The model distinguishes individual redress delay from platform sanction delay. Longer individual delay raises the take rate at which contestation becomes privately worthwhile, while longer platform-side delay weakens the present value of deterrence. The paper then introduces an ex-ante acceptance constraint generated by delegated representation. A representative that can search, compare, negotiate, reject, or redirect transactions can improve the user’s net executable outside option and tighten the platform’s feasible take, provided that refusal is technologically and contractually executable. The effect of AI-based representation is therefore conditional on interoperability, economically meaningful portability, switching feasibility, fiduciary alignment, bounded authorization, and contestability of the agent layer itself. The framework integrates contestation, deterrence, and refusal within a single model of digital-market contestability and derives empirical strategies for separately identifying the effects of redress delay, sanction delay, and ex-ante outside-option improvements.
