Reputation Portability and Endogenous Recognition in Platform Competition

Chang-Gyu Kwag · UCAISM Global Initiative

Research role
Industrial-organization model of reputation portability, endogenous recognition, and platform contestability

Abstract

Digital labor platforms make reputation valuable but platform-specific. Portability rights allow workers to export ratings, yet a destination platform may recognize imported reputation only imperfectly. This paper models an entrant that invests in recognizing imported reputation and an incumbent that chooses costly source-side recognition friction before the two platforms compete in fees for workers with heterogeneous switching costs. Effective recognition shifts workers and operating profit toward the entrant and compresses the fee gap; these effects extend to log-concave switching-cost distributions when the equilibrium switching threshold is interior. Under stated concavity and monotonicity conditions, the recognition stage has a unique equilibrium in which recognition and friction are strategically linked. Raising the cost of friction crowds in recognition, whereas stronger formal portability raises incumbent friction in the interior region, so equilibrium effective recognition can rise less than proportionally or even fall. Purely strategic friction is socially excessive in a first-best benchmark and, under a stated condition, at the market allocation. Formal portability and effective recognition are therefore distinct policy objects.
